Tanzu Chat Faceplate

A minimal visual system for Tanzu Platform Chat, built for an operations audience: calm surfaces, generous margins, and state you can read at a glance — without relying on colour, and without a single red-versus-green judgement anywhere in the interface.

Every control states what it does. The send button carries the word Send and matches the field's height exactly; the failed server offers Retry connection in place of its tool list, because that is the only thing worth doing to it. Destinations are square and full-bleed — the highlight is the whole row, with no unhighlighted gutter between neighbours — and the current one is filled rather than merely tinted. Below 600px the rail becomes a bottom bar and panels arrive as sheets from the bottom, matching where the tap happened rather than sliding in from the side.

Principles

Colour is the third channel

Shape first, word second, colour third. Every state is a distinct silhouette and a plain-English label before it is a hue, which satisfies WCAG 1.4.1 by construction rather than by audit.

Say what the control does

No icon-only actions in the primary flow. Labels are verbs in the user's language — Add document, Retry connection — and every target clears 44 px.

One accent, spent carefully

A single viridian carries brand, primary action, and the healthy state. Amber and violet appear only when something needs attention, so noticing them means something.

Room is a feature

Panels sit on an 8 px grid with 24 px interior gutters and a 62-character measure for prose. Density is reserved for machine strings, where scanning beats reading.

Colour · measured, not asserted

The accent descends from the product's existing mint seed. The signal hues do not: they were chosen by simulating protanopia, deuteranopia and tritanopia, then keeping the trio with the largest worst-case separation.

Simulated with the Viénot–Brettel dichromat matrices; separation is CIE76 ΔE, where anything above 20 reads as clearly different. The first trio tried here — teal, amber and plum — collapsed to ΔE 8 under deuteranopia. Violet replaced plum for that reason alone.

Why the pastels are decoration

tints under protanopia — ΔE 8 to 22

Pastel tints are low-chroma by definition, so under simulation the mint and wheat backgrounds land within ΔE 8 of each other — indistinguishable. They are surfaces that make the interface calm; they are never the thing carrying the message. That job belongs to the ink, the silhouette and the label.

Icons · one enrichment each

A bespoke set on a 24-unit grid, 1.6 px strokes, round joins, drawn in currentColor so a glyph inherits whatever state it sits in. Each carries one extra detail that encodes something true about this product rather than a generic category.

Components

Pills

28 px minimum height, fully round, 1 px border at 26% of the ink. Icon then label, always in that order.

Controls

One primary action per view. Secondary actions are outlined; tertiary are quiet until hovered. Nothing important is icon-only.

Focus is a 2 px accent ring at 2 px offset — never removed, visible on both grounds. Tab through this page to see it.

Containers

10 px radius, one hairline border, no drop shadows — depth comes from the border and a tinted ground, which keeps the interface flat enough to stay legible at high contrast. Interior padding 24 px on panels, 12 px on nested cards, 8 px between siblings.

The one rename that matters: error is violet. Nothing in this interface is red, because red against green is the single most common thing a colour-blind operator cannot resolve — and this product asks people to judge health at a glance all day.

Contrast ratios are WCAG 2.1 relative luminance; CVD figures are CIE76 ΔE on Viénot–Brettel simulations. Every pair shown passes AA, and all but one passes AAA.
